# Rich Email Style Guide

## Message Shape

Use this order unless the audience needs something simpler:

1. Topic H1
2. One-sentence context
3. Executive-summary callout
4. Findings or status
5. What changed or what is requested
6. Decision boundary, risk, or next step
7. Verification or evidence

## Density

- Aim for 300-700 words.
- Keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences.
- Use 3-6 bullets per section.
- Use at most four H2 sections after the H1.
- Use tables for comparison or status, not prose.
- Keep tables to two columns when possible and no more than eight body rows.

## Formatting

- Use bold only for decision words, labels, and short lead-ins.
- Use one informational callout for the executive summary.
- Use a second callout only for a material boundary, risk, or required action.
- Prefer descriptive links such as "Open the production screening page."
- Do not use decorative banners, gradients, or embedded remote images.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate status.

## Content Quality

- Lead with what changed and why it matters.
- Separate evidence from recommendation.
- State unresolved decisions explicitly.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives and diagnostic claims.
- Include exact release identifiers or source links when they help verification.
- Do not expose secrets, tokens, private intake data, or item-level clinical answers.

## Signature Handling

Do not include a generated signoff. New Outlook places its default signature above the imported rich content, separated by two blank lines. Before sending, the user can delete that signature or move it below the content.

## Acceptance Check

- The first screen communicates the topic and outcome.
- The requested action is obvious.
- Tables fit at typical Outlook reading width.
- Links are readable and correct.
- The default Outlook signature is separated from the rich content by two blank lines.
- No generated signoff duplicates the Outlook signature.
- Plain-text paste remains understandable if HTML formatting is stripped.
